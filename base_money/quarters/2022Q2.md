## What's left out

Nothing: all twelve basket issuers are counted, so coverage is 100%. Brazil's figure is the BCB's restricted monetary base (SGS 1788), used as published; it leaves out banks' compulsory deposits on time and savings deposits and their remunerated voluntary deposits at the BCB, and the exigibilidades for funds not directed to rural and housing credit (see the [BCB README](../issuers/BCB/README.md#what-we-measure)). Hong Kong's figure is the HKMA's own monetary base (Certificates of Indebtedness, government notes and coins, the Aggregate Balance and Exchange Fund Bills and Notes), end of day 30 June, used as published (see the [HKMA README](../issuers/HKMA/README.md#what-we-measure)). The UAE's figure is the CBUAE's own monetary base (Currency Issued, banks' reserve balances, current accounts and overnight deposits at the CBUAE, and Monetary Bills & Islamic Certificates of Deposit), end of June, used as published (see the [CBUAE README](../issuers/CBUAE/README.md#what-we-measure)). Taiwan's figure is the CBC's own reserve money (reserves held by other MFIs and currency held by the public), end of June, used as published (see the [CBC README](../issuers/CBC/README.md#what-we-measure)).

**India's count date is 24 June 2022, not 30 June**: before 15 December 2025 the RBI published reserve money for Fridays only, and it publishes a final quarter-end figure only for 31 March, its fiscal year-end, so under the [RBI count-date rule](../../docs/methodology/base_money.md#per-issuer-definitions) the last Friday on or before the quarter end, 24 June 2022, is used (30 June 2022 was a Thursday). The figure comes from the supplement of 1 July 2022 and is observed, not adjusted to quarter end.

**The euro area's statement date is 24 June 2022, not 30 June**: the Eurosystem's weekly statement is dated Fridays, so under the [ECB note](../../docs/methodology/base_money.md#per-issuer-definitions) the last statement dated on or before the quarter end, as at 24 June 2022 (published 28 June), is used; the next one, as at 1 July 2022, is after the quarter end.

**Hong Kong's count date is 30 June 2022**: 30 June 2022 was a Thursday and not a Hong Kong general holiday (HKSAR Establishment Day was the next day, 1 July), so under the [HKMA count-date rule](../../docs/methodology/base_money.md#per-issuer-definitions) the quarter-end date itself is used.

**Growth rates.** [2022Q3](2022Q3.md) also counts all twelve issuers: its total ($24,005,016,053,758) is $1,665,132,165,207 or 6.487% below this quarter's; part of that is exchange rates, since each balance is converted at the market rate on its own count date. This quarter is the year-ago base for [2023Q2](2023Q2.md) ($24,443,794,122,229), which is $1,226,354,096,736 or 4.777% below this quarter's.

## Checks and possible conflicts

No source disagrees with an adopted figure. The UAE figure was revised down from its first print, and a later RBI supplement implies a slightly higher figure for India's count date.

- **India.** The adopted figure (4,103,867 ₹ crore on 24 June) is the printed total; its printed components add up to 4,103,866, 1 below it, a rounding difference in the published components. It is consistent within the same supplement: the 31 March 2022 column (4,068,887) plus the printed 2022-23 "Financial Year so far" change (34,980) gives 4,103,867. The next supplement (1 July 2022 figures) prints 4,111,721 with a week change of 7,854, which implies 4,103,867 for 24 June, equal to the adopted figure. The RBI may have revised this Friday's figure later: the 30 June 2023 supplement's year-on-year and 2022-23 financial-year-so-far columns both imply 4,104,025 for 24 June 2022, 158 crore (0.004%) above the adopted 4,103,867. No supplement found prints a revised level, so we keep the figure first published, in the 1 July 2022 supplement, which is final and unmarked; the later implied value changes no displayed YoY (2023Q2: +6.5% either way) ([extract](../issuers/RBI/2022.md#rbi-reserve-money-2022-06-24-wss-2022-07-01)).
- **Euro area.** The ECB press release headline, EUR 6,241.1 billion, matches the summed items (EUR 6,241,105 million) ([extract](../issuers/ECB/2022.md#ecb-press-release-base-money-headline-statement-2022-06-24-published-2022-06-28)).
- **Russia.** The CBR's narrow monetary base (14,140.2 billion rubles on 1 July 2022) is a different definition, not a conflict ([extract](../issuers/CBR/2022.md#cbr-monetary-base-narrow-definition-01072022-retrieved-2026-11-10)); its printed total is 0.1 below its printed components (14,033.3 + 107.0 = 14,140.3), a rounding difference. The two tables differ on required reserves (107.0 narrow vs 145.9 broad); the CBR's footnotes don't explain it, as in the other quarters. Currency issued on 1 July 2022 (14,033.3) is the same in both tables, which confirms the broad "Jun, 2022" row is the stock at the start of 1 July. The day before the start of 1 July, 30 June, was a Thursday with Moscow Exchange trading, so the rate is that day's close (`prior close`). The ruble rate (53.52967495 per USD, Moscow Exchange CNY/RUB × USD/CNY, per the FX rule) is **1.328% below** the TradingView ICE USD/RUB close for the same day (54.25), the largest FX gap this quarter. Both legs are from the same day (the USD/CNY leg is the CNY row's 30 June close, 6.6995). The Moscow Exchange's own USD/RUB (`USDRUB_TOM`, board CETS) closed at 51.45 on 30 June 2022, 5.16% below TradingView, and the cross rate is 4.04% above that close ([rate](../FX/2022.md#fx-2022q2-rub)). Converted at the TradingView close instead, Russia's figure would be $353,321,658,986 rather than $358,076,151,553. The product's standing rule is the CNY/RUB MOEX cross, so the MarketWatch-leg cross is used and the gap is flagged.
- **Brazil.** SGS 1788 end-of-June 2022 is 407,168,900 (R$ thousand). SGS 20306 "Reserve Money - Monetary Authority", BRL 887,443,000,000, is an alternative definition that no BCB primary source defines, never summed ([extract](../issuers/BCB/2022.md#bcb-sgs-20306-reserve-money---monetary-authority-jun2022-sgs-json-api-retrieved-2026-11-10)).
- **Hong Kong.** Table 7.2.1 of the HKMA's Monthly Statistical Bulletin (end-of-period figures) prints the same 2,048,462 (HK$ million) for 2022-06 as the adopted daily figure for 2022-06-30, with the same four components (cited in the [extract](../issuers/HKMA/2022.md#hkma-total-monetary-base-before-discount-window-2022-06-30-open-api-daily-figures-retrieved-2026-11-10)); a cross-check, never summed.
- **UAE.** The adopted figure (466,044 AED million, Jun 2022, Statistical Bulletin August 2024, the latest bulletin still served by the CBUAE that prints June 2022) is the printed total and equals its four printed components. The June 2022 bulletin first printed 470,744, marked "* Preliminary", with the same Currency Issued, Reserve Requirements and Monetary Bills but current accounts and overnight deposits of 93,647 rather than 88,947. The September 2022 bulletin, and later bulletins through August 2023, still printed 470,744. The September 2023 bulletin is the earliest that prints the revised 466,044 (current accounts and overnight deposits 88,947), and it footnotes that row "Revised to account for amendments in Overnight Deposit Facility data"; later bulletins through August 2024 keep 466,044 unmarked. The later, unmarked figure is used under the [CBUAE vintage rule](../../docs/methodology/base_money.md#per-issuer-definitions). The June 2022, August 2023 and September 2023 bulletins are read cross-checks, never summed (cited in the [extract](../issuers/CBUAE/2022.md#cbuae-monetary-base-jun-2022-statistical-bulletin-august-2024-table-4-retrieved-2026-11-10)).
- **Taiwan.** The adopted figure (5,403,691 NT$ millions, 2022M06, EF12M01en) is the printed total and equals its two printed subtotals; the CBC's English Excel table ERM-H prints 54037 (NT$ hundred millions) for 2022M06, the same figure rounded ([extract](../issuers/CBC/2022.md#cbc-reserve-money-2022-06-30-ef12m01en-end-of-month-retrieved-2026-11-10)). The TWD rate (29.7253 per USD) is **0.200% above** the TradingView ICE close for the same day (29.666), the second-largest FX gap this quarter; the MarketWatch close is used, per the FX rule.
- **Rounding in printed totals.** Every printed total used equals its components except India's (components 1 crore below the printed total, noted above). The BCB's SGS JSON shows no components; the ECB and BoE figures are exact sums with no printed total. The CBR's narrow table, not adopted, is 0.1 below its components (noted above).
- **FX: MarketWatch daily closes (FX rule amendment 2026-10-28).** Yahoo Finance serves hourly bars only for the last 730 days, so every rate here is the MarketWatch daily close (Tullett Prebon via FactSet) for its trading date, one by-date link per rate, archived on the Wayback Machine (month averages: the mean of 22 weekday closes, 1 to 30 June). MarketWatch does not state its close time (about 20:00–22:00 UTC). The quarter-end date 30 June 2022 was a Thursday, so every single-day rate dated on it is that day's close; the EUR and INR rates are the 24 June closes, the dates of the ECB statement and the RBI count. 2 and 3 June were UK bank holidays (Spring bank holiday and Platinum Jubilee), so under the holiday rule both take 1 June's close in the GBP month average; 6 June (Whit Monday) takes 3 June's close in the CHF average; 16 June (Corpus Christi) takes 15 June's close in the BRL average. The holiday calendars are cited in the [FX notes](../FX/2022.md#2022q2). Gaps to the TradingView ICE cross-check, flagged here since there is no divergence limit: RUB -1.328%; TWD +0.200%; CHF +0.055%; EUR +0.038%; INR +0.026%; BRL +0.025%; JPY -0.022%; GBP +0.020%; AED +0.011%; HKD +0.009%; CNY +0.004%. Converted at the TradingView closes instead, the total would be $25,662,738,722,202, 0.029% below the adopted total.
- **Other aggregates.** None compared this quarter.
