# Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA)

- **Official name:** Reserve Bank of Australia
- **Country or currency area:** Australia
- **Currency:** Australian dollar, AUD
- **What it is:** Australia's central bank. It issues Australia's banknotes; the Royal Australian Mint produces the circulating coins. It publishes the money base in its monetary aggregates (statistical table D3).
- **Official website:** [rba.gov.au](https://www.rba.gov.au/)
- **Data pages used here:** statistical table D3 "Monetary Aggregates", one CSV at a fixed URL ([d3-data.csv](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/d3-data.csv), archived: [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261120161450/https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/d3-data.csv)); each figure's exact row is in the year files ([2020](2020.md), [2021](2021.md), [2022](2022.md), [2023](2023.md), [2024](2024.md), [2025](2025.md), [2026](2026.md))

## What we measure

D3 series `DMAMMB` "Money base" ($ billion, monthly, Original, not seasonally adjusted), the row for the quarter-end month, used as published. The notes to table D3 ([d03hist.xlsx](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/xls/d03hist.xlsx), sheet Notes, archived: [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261120161526/https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/xls/d03hist.xlsx)) define it:

- "‘Money base’ is defined as holdings of notes and coins by the private sector plus deposits of banks with the Reserve Bank and other Reserve Bank liabilities to the private non-bank sector."

The RBA's March 2019 Bulletin article "Updates to Australia's Financial Aggregates" ([Table 2](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2019/mar/updates-to-australias-financial-aggregates.html), archived: [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261120161640/https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2019/mar/updates-to-australias-financial-aggregates.html)) gives the same measure as "Currency + banks' holdings of notes and coins + deposits of banks with the RBA + other RBA liabilities to the private non-bank sector", where currency is "Holdings of notes and coins by the private non-bank sector".

**Monthly average (inferred).** The RBA does not say whether money base is an end-of-month stock or an average over the month. Table A1 ([a1-data.csv](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/a1-data.csv), archived: [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261120161505/https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/a1-data.csv)), the RBA's weekly balance sheet, fits an average: in every quarter-end month from March 2020 to September 2026, money base less the mean of the month's Wednesday figures for notes on issue plus exchange settlement balances is 3.3 to 5.1 billion, while money base less the last Wednesday's figures ranges from -46.3 to +40.8 billion. See [Per-issuer definitions](../../../docs/methodology/base_money.md#per-issuer-definitions).

**Not used.** The other D3 series (currency, M1, M3, broad money and their seasonally adjusted forms) are not base money. Table A1 is evidence for the averaging only and is never summed.

## What's in, what's out

- In: notes and coins held by the private sector (including banks' holdings of notes and coins), deposits of banks with the RBA, and other RBA liabilities to the private non-bank sector.
- Out: government deposits and foreign official deposits at the RBA (inferred: not listed in the definition); the RBA issues no securities that the definition counts.
- Coin: produced and issued by the Royal Australian Mint, counted inside "notes and coins".

See [Where the banks' definitions differ](../../../docs/methodology/base_money.md#where-the-banks-definitions-differ).
